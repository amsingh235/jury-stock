---
title: Thinking, Fast and Slow
author: Kahneman
year: 2011
doc_type: book
school: behavioral_risk
---
PLACEHOLDER knowledge note. System 1 is fast and intuitive, System 2 slow and deliberate. Biases include anchoring, availability, representativeness, overconfidence, loss aversion and the narrative fallacy (WYSIATI: what you see is all there is). Experts in low-validity environments such as stock picking are prone to an illusion of skill.

---
title: Irrational Exuberance
author: Shiller
year: 2000
doc_type: book
school: behavioral_risk
---
PLACEHOLDER knowledge note. Asset bubbles are driven by feedback loops: rising prices create stories and attention, which attract more buyers. High valuation ratios (CAPE) predict lower long-run returns. Media, new-era thinking and herd behaviour amplify booms.

---
title: Fooled by Randomness
author: Taleb
year: 2001
doc_type: book
school: behavioral_risk
---
PLACEHOLDER knowledge note. Many apparent successes reflect luck and survivorship; we see winners but not the graveyard. Rare, extreme events dominate outcomes, and strategies that earn steady small gains can carry hidden blow-up risk. Track records must be judged against the alternative histories that could have occurred.

---
title: Trading Is Hazardous to Your Wealth
author: Barber and Odean
year: 2000
doc_type: paper
school: behavioral_risk
---
PLACEHOLDER knowledge note. Among US retail brokerage households, those who traded most earned an annual net return around 11.4 percent versus 18.5 percent for the market. Overtrading driven by overconfidence and costs destroys returns.

---
title: Boys Will Be Boys: Gender, Overconfidence, and Common Stock Investment
author: Barber and Odean
year: 2001
doc_type: paper
school: behavioral_risk
---
PLACEHOLDER knowledge note. Men traded about 45 percent more than women and reduced their net returns more, consistent with overconfidence. Overconfidence leads to excessive trading and poor results.

---
title: Herd Behavior and Investment
author: Scharfstein and Stein
year: 1990
doc_type: paper
school: behavioral_risk
---
PLACEHOLDER knowledge note. Managers concerned with reputation rationally mimic others and ignore private information. Herding produces correlated errors and amplifies price moves away from fundamentals.

---
title: The Disposition to Sell Winners Too Early and Ride Losers Too Long
author: Shefrin and Statman
year: 1985
doc_type: paper
school: behavioral_risk
---
PLACEHOLDER knowledge note. Investors are reluctant to realise losses and eager to realise gains, consistent with prospect theory and mental accounting. The disposition effect creates overhang that can slow price adjustment.

---
title: Data-Snooping, Technical Trading Rule Performance, and the Bootstrap
author: Sullivan, Timmermann and White
year: 1999
doc_type: paper
school: behavioral_risk
---
PLACEHOLDER knowledge note. When thousands of technical rules are tested, some will look profitable by chance. White's reality check corrects for data snooping; after correction, the best-performing rules on the Dow lose significance out of sample.

---
title: Survivorship Bias in Performance Studies
author: Brown, Goetzmann, Ibbotson and Ross
year: 1992
doc_type: paper
school: behavioral_risk
---
PLACEHOLDER knowledge note. Excluding dead funds or delisted stocks creates spurious persistence and inflates average returns. Any backtest on today's listed universe overstates performance, especially for small and penny stocks with high delisting rates.

---
title: The Black Swan
author: Taleb
year: 2007
doc_type: book
school: behavioral_risk
---
PLACEHOLDER knowledge note. Black swans are rare, high-impact, retrospectively predictable events. Fat tails mean standard deviation understates risk. Fragile positions such as illiquid, leveraged or operator-driven stocks should be assumed exposed to sudden collapse.
