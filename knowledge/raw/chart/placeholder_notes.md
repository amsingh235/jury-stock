title: Technical Analysis of Stock Trends
author: Edwards and Magee
year: 1948
doc_type: book
school: price_action
---
PLACEHOLDER knowledge note. Prices move in trends that persist until clear reversal signals appear. Reversal patterns (head and shoulders, double and triple tops and bottoms) and continuation patterns (triangles, flags, pennants) reflect shifts in supply and demand. Volume should confirm a breakout; breakouts on low volume are suspect. Support broken often becomes resistance and vice versa.

---
title: Technical Analysis of the Financial Markets
author: Murphy
year: 1999
doc_type: book
school: price_action
---
PLACEHOLDER knowledge note. Murphy's three premises: market action discounts everything, prices move in trends, history repeats. Trend is identified by higher highs and higher lows; moving averages smooth noise, and crossovers signal change. Oscillators such as RSI and MACD are most useful in trading ranges and for divergences. Confluence of independent signals across timeframes increases reliability.

---
title: Encyclopedia of Chart Patterns
author: Bulkowski
year: 2005
doc_type: book
school: price_action
---
PLACEHOLDER knowledge note. Bulkowski catalogues thousands of patterns with measured performance. Typical stats he reports (approximate, bull markets, daily data): head-and-shoulders top failure rate around 19 percent to reach its target; double bottom (Adam and Adam) breakout failure around 11-16 percent; ascending triangle upward breakouts fail around 13 percent; bull flags fail around 4-12 percent depending on variant; cup with handle failure around 5-14 percent. Patterns that break out in the direction of the primary trend perform better. Exact figures must be checked against the source.

---
title: Encyclopedia of Candlestick Charts
author: Bulkowski
year: 2008
doc_type: book
school: price_action
---
PLACEHOLDER knowledge note. Most candlestick patterns perform close to random; only a minority show reliable reversal or continuation behaviour. Performance improves when the pattern appears at a significant support or resistance level and in the context of the prevailing trend. Single candles should never be used alone.

---
title: What Do We Know About the Profitability of Technical Analysis?
author: Park and Irwin
year: 2007
doc_type: paper
school: price_action
---
PLACEHOLDER knowledge note. A survey of technical-analysis studies finds that most modern studies report positive profits, especially in foreign exchange and futures, but many suffer from data snooping, ex-post rule selection and poor accounting for costs and risk. Profitability appears to have declined over time.

---
title: Simple Technical Trading Rules and the Stochastic Properties of Stock Returns
author: Brock, Lakonishok and LeBaron
year: 1992
doc_type: paper
school: price_action
---
PLACEHOLDER knowledge note. Moving-average crossover and trading-range-breakout rules on the Dow Jones index (1897-1986) produced buy signals followed by higher returns and lower volatility than sell signals. Bootstrapped null models could not reproduce these results. Later studies show the effect weakened after transaction costs and in later decades.

---
title: New Concepts in Technical Trading Systems
author: Wilder
year: 1978
doc_type: book
school: price_action
---
PLACEHOLDER knowledge note. Wilder introduces RSI (14-period, overbought above 70, oversold below 30), Average True Range for volatility, the Directional Movement system with ADX (above 25 indicates a trending market) and Parabolic SAR for trailing stops. Wilder smoothing uses a 1/n exponential average.

---
title: MACD and Volume-Price Confirmation
author: Appel; Arditti
year: 1979
doc_type: notes
school: price_action
---
PLACEHOLDER knowledge note. Appel's MACD is the 12-period EMA minus the 26-period EMA with a 9-period signal line; crossovers and zero-line crosses indicate momentum change and divergences warn of weakening trends. Volume-price studies (Arditti 1967 and later literature) indicate that price moves on rising volume carry more information than moves on falling volume.

---
title: Candlestick Technical Trading Strategies: Can They Create Value for Investors?
author: Marshall, Young and Rose
year: 2006
doc_type: paper
school: price_action
---
PLACEHOLDER knowledge note. Testing single, double and triple candlestick reversal patterns on Dow Jones Industrial Average stocks (1992-2002) with bootstrap methods, the authors find candlestick signals have no useful predictive power once compared with random strategies. Candlesticks alone should not be trusted; any read needs confirmation from trend, support/resistance or volume.

---
title: Pattern Recognition and Candlestick Profitability in Taiwan
author: Lu, Shiu and Liu
year: 2012
doc_type: paper
school: price_action
---
PLACEHOLDER knowledge note. In the Taiwan stock market, some two-day bullish reversal patterns (such as bullish engulfing and bullish harami) remained profitable after transaction costs, while many other patterns did not. Candlestick predictive value appears market- and period-specific, stronger in emerging, retail-heavy markets, and decays as patterns become widely known.

---
title: The Predictive Power of Price Patterns
author: Caginalp and Laurent
year: 1998
doc_type: paper
school: price_action
---
PLACEHOLDER knowledge note. Testing eight three-day candlestick reversal patterns on S&P 500 stocks (1992-1996), the authors report statistically significant predictive power, with average returns after down-to-up reversal patterns materially above the baseline over a few days. The result supports candlesticks as short-horizon signals, though later studies found weaker evidence after costs.

---
title: A New Anomaly: The Cross-Sectional Profitability of Technical Analysis
author: Han, Yang and Zhou
year: 2013
doc_type: paper
school: price_action
---
PLACEHOLDER knowledge note. A simple moving-average timing strategy applied to portfolios sorted by volatility earns abnormal returns that rise with volatility, and the gains are largest for the most volatile, hardest-to-value stocks. Technical signals are most informative where information uncertainty is highest, a condition common among small, low-priced stocks.

---
title: Technical Analysis: An Asset Allocation Perspective on the Use of Moving Averages
author: Zhu and Zhou
year: 2009
doc_type: paper
school: price_action
---
PLACEHOLDER knowledge note. When returns are predictable and investors are uncertain about the predictive process, moving-average rules can add value to a fixed allocation rule. The benefit is greatest when the underlying process is persistent and the investor faces parameter uncertainty, giving a theoretical rationale for trend-following via moving averages.
